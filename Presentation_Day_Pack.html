5. Questions the judges are likely to ask

Short answers, true to the prototype as it is today. Pick an owner for each group tonight (suggestion: users and problem: Speakers 1 and 2; ideas: 3; demo and technology: 4; privacy, business and close: 5). If you do not know, say so and say how you would find out.

The problem and the people

Is this a real problem, or one you assumed?

It comes from our own daily experience at this gate. We have no formal gate data, and we say so on slide 3. A pilot at one gate would measure how often students are stopped and how long it takes.

Did you interview guards and administrators?

Agree one true answer tonight. The empathy-map slides say the personas come from the team's experience and research, not interview data. If you had informal talks, say "informal conversations", not "interviews". Either way: "validating with real guards and administrators is our next step."

Who pays, and who is the customer?

The college administration. Students use it on phones they already own. The scanner is one phone or tablet at the gate. We have not priced the running cost yet: it is a small server plan and an email service.

How is this different from APAAR ID, or a campus ID in Apple Wallet?

APAAR is a national academic-record ID, not built for gate access (slide 4 table). Apple Wallet campus IDs exist abroad and need specific phones. Ours is a gate pass for an Indian college that works on any Android camera and verifies offline.

Why a changing QR instead of a plain QR or barcode?

A static code can be screenshotted and shared. Ours is replaced every 30 seconds and signed by a key inside the phone, so a copy is useless after about 45 seconds.

Security and fraud

Can someone forward a screenshot of the QR?

It works for at most about 45 seconds (30 s refresh, a 45 s window). After that the scanner says Clock mismatch. We can show it.

Can the pass be copied to another phone?

The pass is bound to a key made inside the phone's Android Keystore (secure hardware where the phone has it), and the key cannot be exported. Every QR needs a fresh signature from that key, so a copy without the key is refused. A second phone for the same student replaces the first.

What stops a student lending their phone to a friend?

Honest gap. The scanner shows name, roll number, branch and year, and the guard checks the person against them. Showing the student's photo on the scanner is the next step (the photo stays on the server and is not in the QR, by design), and so is an app lock. Do not claim the scanner shows a photo today.

Can a pass be forged?

Not without the college's private signing key. In the prototype that key is a public test key, so anyone who reads our repository could forge one. A real launch generates a new key and keeps it in a cloud key vault.

What if the scanner phone is lost or stolen?

It holds only the public key, so it cannot create passes. It syncs with a token from a one-time activation code, and a new code replaces the old token. Its saved list is not encrypted yet and holds only two days.

What if a student leaves, or loses their phone? Can you cancel a pass?

The server records revocations and a new phone gets a new pass. The signed revocation list and its checks are in the format and tested. The scanner app does not download the list yet, so a cancelled pass works until it expires (up to 180 days). That is our first item before a pilot.

How do you know the person signing up is really a student?

They must own the college mailbox roll-number@vnrvjiet.in and enter the one-time code. The college issues those emails, so owning one is proof of enrolment, and the admin can still reject someone. Today no email is sent: the code is shown in the app, so the proof is simulated. The ID card scan is a convenience, not a check.

Why no admin approval for each student?

The administrator told us, in effect, "no new list to maintain". Email ownership gives automatic approval, with an admin override and flags for duplicate photos.

Privacy and law

What data do you store?

Name, roll number, branch, year, college email, phone number and one profile photo. The ID card photo is read on the phone and deleted. The QR itself carries name, roll number, branch, year, pass type, a short photo fingerprint and a public key: no phone number and no photo.

Who can see what?

The student sees their own pass. A guard sees the result and today's list on their own phone, view only. Only the admin, signed in to the website, can search everything and export. The admin site has one shared password and no separate accounts or audit trail yet.

Does it comply with the DPDP Act?

We designed for lawful purpose, consent and minimal data (slide 4), but we have not done a formal compliance review. A consent screen and retention rules are on the list: the guard's phone already drops entries after two days.

Technology and scale

How can it verify with no internet?

The scanner has the college's public key and checks two signatures on the phone itself. Exits are saved on the phone first and synced later: in tests and on a real phone, scans made offline reached the server later with their original times.

Will it cope with the nine o'clock rush?

Not yet measured. Verification is local maths with no network wait, and the QR is kept small (about 340 characters) for fast scans. We have not tested a real rush, sunlight or gate distance. Measuring that is the purpose of a one-gate pilot.

What if the scanner's clock or the student's clock is wrong?

The window is tight on purpose, so a badly wrong clock gives Clock mismatch. Both apps correct for drift against the server when they can.

Android only? What about iPhones?

The prototype is Android 8 and above. The format is platform neutral; an iPhone version would keep the same P-256 key in the Secure Enclave. Not built.

What if the server is down?

The gate keeps working: checking is offline and exits queue on the phone. New sign-ups and renewals need the server (the app asks for renewal 14 days before the pass expires).

What if a student's battery is dead?

Not built. Today they go to the backup desk. Manual entry by roll number is designed in our spec but not in the app yet.

How do you know it works?

780 automated tests pass (305 server, 68 credential, 7 shared UI, 150 student app, 250 scanner). Three independent implementations (Node, TypeScript, Dart) produce identical signatures on shared test vectors. A full test runs the whole loop against a real server: activate a scanner, sign up, get a pass, scan, sync, see the exit in the admin export. It has been seen on a real Android phone. Not yet tried: phone to phone, daylight, gate distance.

Is it running in the cloud? Why Flutter and Supabase?

No: it runs on this laptop, using the same handlers and SQL as the planned Supabase Edge Functions, on a real Postgres. Flutter gives one codebase for both apps. ES256 is what Android's secure hardware supports natively.

Business and next steps

How would you roll it out?

Pilot at one gate with one batch, interview guards and administrators, measure scan time and failures, then add gates and later uses (library, exam hall, canteen). The format already reserves a temporary pass for freshers and a visitor pass type.

What would you build next, in order?

1. Real email, Supabase deployment and a real signing key. 2. Revocation list at the scanner. 3. Guard PIN and manual entry. 4. App lock and screenshot blocking. 5. Admin accounts, roles and an audit trail. 6. The one-gate pilot.

Is the face match built?

No. We removed it from the claims. The guard compares the person with the details shown. Automatic face matching is a later option, and storing no face templates is better for privacy.
